◆ The High-Stakes Initiative Operating System

Every initiative I’ve led has needed the same four steps in the same order, because each step only works if the one before it is done. The full process typically runs 90 to 120 days.

  1. Diagnosis

    Phase 1 · Diagnosis

    Step 1: Get the Real Picture
    Step 2: Surface the Elephants

    I stress-test the business case, talk with intended customers or users, and meet one-on-one with the team and the stakeholders who’ll decide whether it gets adopted.

    What you get:

    • An assessment of what’s working, what’s broken, and what needs to change
    • Explicit success criteria and the assumptions the business case rests on
    • A stakeholder map, and the concerns nobody is raising in the meeting
    • A short list of early possible wins
  2. Alignment

    Phase 2 · Alignment

    Step 3: Align the Plans

    I test the product, technology, and rollout plans against the business case and against each other, and we resolve what doesn’t line up.

    What you get:

    • Product, technology, and rollout strategies aligned to the business case
    • The gaps between them, and a plan to close each one
    • The decisions that need senior attention, surfaced and made
  3. Hand-off

    Phase 3 · Hand-off

    Step 4: Install the Operating System

    We run the operating system together until the initiative has momentum, then I hand it to a named successor.

    What you get:

    • Decision, risk, and assumption registers
    • A weekly operating meeting, a sponsor escalation meeting, and a stakeholder communication rhythm
    • A named owner and a plan for after the engagement ends
